## Wait, which Base Layer is this?

Fair question, and the reason this page exists. If you searched "base layer moisturizer," you found two different companies.

**Caldera + Lab** is a premium men's skincare brand out of Jackson Hole, Wyoming. One of their products, a rich moisturizer, is called The Base Layer. It costs $65 for 1.7 oz.

**Base Layer** (that's us) is a men's skincare brand out of Breckenridge, Colorado. We make one product: a daily face cream with disclosed active percentages. It costs $38 for 50 mL, which is also 1.7 oz.

Two mountain-town brands, one name, same bottle size, very different philosophies. Let's get into the actual differences.

## Who Caldera + Lab is best for

Credit where it's due. Caldera + Lab is a serious operation, not a dropship label. Their products are MADE SAFE certified, vegan, and built on an unusually deep botanical stack. The Base Layer's ingredient list opens with sea silt extract and runs through tremella mushroom (a humectant), oat kernel extract (a soother), gluconolactone (a gentle PHA exfoliant, genuinely nice to see in a moisturizer), and something like twenty plant extracts.

Their PDP reports strong consumer perception results: "94% agree skin shows an overall younger-looking appearance" and "91% agree fine lines and wrinkles appear diminished." Read those for what they are. "Agree" statistics come from asking panelists how their skin looks, not from instrument measurements. That's standard practice in skincare marketing, theirs and everyone else's, but it's worth knowing what the number means.

Caldera positions The Base Layer for "dry skin, cold or dry climates, skin that needs nourishment." That tracks with the formula: cetearyl alcohol and rich emollients high in the list make it a heavier cream than ours. If you have genuinely dry skin in a cold climate and you want a luxurious, botanical-first product, it's a defensible pick.

## Where Caldera + Lab falls short

Three things, and none of them are "it's bad."

**1. No disclosed concentrations.** Not one ingredient on The Base Layer's list comes with a percentage. Plant stem cells and botanical extracts can do real work at the right dose and nothing at a trace dose, and the label gives you no way to know which you're getting. When a brand knows an active is at an effective concentration, they usually say so, loudly.

**2. Added fragrance.** The INCI includes Fragrance/Parfum and citronellol, a declared fragrance allergen. Plenty of men will never notice. But fragrance is one of the most common causes of allergic contact dermatitis: in the North American Contact Dermatitis Group's 2019-2020 patch-test data, fragrance mix I produced positive reactions in 12.8% of patients tested, the third most common allergen identified ([PubMed 36917520](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36917520/)). The American Academy of Dermatology likewise advises choosing [fragrance-free products](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/eczema/childhood/triggers/friendly-products) for reactive skin. For a $65 moisturizer aimed at skin that "needs protection," it's a surprising inclusion. If your skin runs reactive, start at our [sensitive skin guide](/skin-concerns/sensitive-skin-men).

**3. The system math.** The Base Layer is designed as one step in Caldera's regimen. Their full Regimen set runs $220, and their subscription reorders The Base Layer alone at $52 every 45 to 75 days. The products may be good. The architecture is built to grow your routine, and your bill, over time. We wrote about [how subscription-first pricing works](/articles/no-subscription-model) and why we run ours differently.

## What Base Layer does differently

We built one product and put the doses on the table.

**Niacinamide at 5%.** The concentration used in most published studies on oil control, barrier function, and tone. Not "contains niacinamide." Five percent. [More on niacinamide](/ingredients/niacinamide).

**Copper peptide GHK-Cu at 0.03%.** A collagen-signaling peptide with real research behind it, at a disclosed working concentration. [More on copper peptides](/ingredients/copper-peptide).

**Hyaluronic acid, squalane, panthenol, centella.** Hydration, barrier support, and irritation control. [Squalane](/ingredients/squalane), notably, is one ingredient both formulas share, so on that axis we're even.

**Fragrance-free, matte finish, 15-second absorb.** Built for guys who want one step that works and gets out of the way, not a ritual.

The philosophical difference is simple. Caldera bets on breadth: twenty botanicals, each contributing something. We bet on depth: five workhorses at doses you can verify against the research. Neither is dishonest. Only one lets you check the math.

## FAQs

**Is Caldera Lab's "The Base Layer" the same as Base Layer?**
No. The Base Layer is a $65 moisturizer made by Caldera + Lab, a Wyoming skincare brand. Base Layer is a Colorado skincare brand whose only product is a $38 daily face cream with niacinamide 5% and copper peptide. Different companies, confusingly similar names.

**Is Caldera Lab legit?**
Yes. MADE SAFE certified, vegan formulas, a real botanical supply chain, and a 60-day guarantee. Our disagreement is with undisclosed concentrations and added fragrance at that price, not with the company's legitimacy.

**Which is better for sensitive skin?**
Base Layer is fragrance-free; Caldera's The Base Layer contains Fragrance/Parfum and citronellol, a declared fragrance allergen. If your skin reacts easily, fragrance-free is the safer default.

**Why is Caldera Lab so expensive?**
You're paying for a large botanical stack, MADE SAFE certification, premium packaging, and a brand built around a multi-step regimen. Whether that's worth $65 per 1.7 oz against a $38 disclosed-actives formula is exactly the judgment this page is for.
